## Tier S

### Blasphemy `skill` `rare` `cost 1` `tier S`

_tags_: divinity, stance

**Effect.** Enter Divinity. Die next turn. Exhaust

**Upgrade.** Retain. Enter Divinity. Die next turn. Exhaust

**Notes.** Although blasphemy might look terrible and frightening at first, the sheer strength of divinity makes this card worth it. When upgraded, you can enter divinity and gain 2 energy by doing so on any turn you like. This makes it a brilliant finisher. It is exceptionally powerful in hallway fights but obviously can be lacklustre in boss battles. Note that the death can be prevented by intangible or buffer, as the death is coded as one large hit of damage. Also note that the blasphemy status cannot be stopped with artifact (since it is actually coded as a buff not a debuff).

**Synergies.** Flurry Of Blows, Flying Sleeves, Mental Fortress, Sands of Time, Windmill Strike, Wreath of Flame, Smite, Through Violence, Toxic Egg, Violet Lotus

**Anti-synergies.** Mayhem, Snecko Eye

### Spirit Shield `skill` `rare` `cost 2` `tier S`

_tags_: block

**Effect.** Gain 3 Block for each card in your hand.

**Upgrade.** Gain 4 Block for each card in your hand.

**Notes.** Spirit shield is one of the strongest cards at the watcher’s disposal. It very consistently generates a lot of block, particularly due to how many of the watcher’s cards have retain. This even includes the miracle that you obtain due to your starting relic. I don't think you can ever have too many copies. 

**Synergies (51).** Deep Breath, Finesse, Flash of Steel, Madness, Master of Strategy, Thinking Ahead, Impatience, Panic Button, The Bomb, Violence, Normality, Battle Hymn, Crescendo, Cut Through Fate, Empty Mind, Evaluate, Flying Sleeves, Inner Peace, Meditate, Perseverance, Protect, Sanctity, Sands of Time, Scrawl, Study, …

**Anti-synergies.** Regret, Letter Opener, Unceasing Top

## Tier S-

### Lesson Learned `attack` `rare` `cost 2` `tier S-`

_tags_: deckupgrade

**Effect.** Deal 10 damage. If Fatal, Upgrade a random card in your deck. Exhaust.

**Upgrade.** Deal 13 damage. If Fatal, Upgrade a random card in your deck. Exhaust.

**Notes.** Better the earlier you get it. Note that the upgrades are permanent, not just for this encounter, and that the encounter does not benefit from the upgrade. If you pick up this card in act one, it is not uncommon to finish with a fully upgraded deck. When you have this card, consider taking more hallway fights than you normally would since it allows you to permanently upgrade a card at low risk. Although it might look silly to upgrade this, if it allows you to more consistently get an upgrade in your deck it is easily worth it. For obvious reasons, this card synergises extremely well with Fusion hammer. 

**★ Super-synergies.** Fusion Hammer

**Synergies.** Madness, Normality, Swivel, Pen Nib, Molten Egg, Snecko Eye, Velvet Choker, Pocketwatch

**Anti-synergies.** Apotheosis, Regret, Signature Move, Kunai, Unceasing Top

## Tier A+

### Foresight `power` `uncommon` `cost 1` `tier A+`

_tags_: scry, innate

**Effect.** At the start of your turn, Scry 3.

**Upgrade.** At the start of your turn, Scry 4.

**Notes.** Foresight is one of the strongest powers the watcher has at her disposal. Although it might look unimpressive, scry allows you to very consistently draw the cards that you want, which becomes even more evident as you begin to learn enemy movesets. It works well with Alpha, as it allows you to quickly sift through your deck and get Omega online. Furthermore, since you scry every single turn, any cards that benefit from a scry effect such as Weave benefit greatly from this card. I would always take one of these over passing.

**★ Super-synergies.** Alpha, Nirvana, Weave

**Synergies.** Signature Move, Beta, Frozen Egg, Mummified Hand, Bird-Faced Urn, Gambling Chip, Gremlin Visage, Golden Eye

**Anti-synergies.** Bag of Marbles, Snecko Eye

## Tier A

### Cut Through Fate `attack` `common` `cost 1` `tier A`

_tags_: scry, draw

**Effect.** Deal 7 damage. Scry 2. Draw 1 card.

**Upgrade.** Deal 9 damage. Scry 3. Draw 1 card.

**Notes.** Cut through fate is an exceptionally strong common card for the watcher. Not only does it provide a draw, but it also allows you two carefully decide which of the top three cards you want to draw. Scrying away strikes and defends happens very frequently, and the scribe is often the difference between drawing enough damage to kill a small creature and not. 

**Synergies.** Panache, Chrysalis, Metamorphosis, Alpha, Deva Form, Spirit Shield, Weave, Beta, Happy Flower, Molten Egg, Frozen Eye, Sundial, The Abacus, Golden Eye, Melange

**Anti-synergies.** Regret, Signature Move, Snecko Eye, Velvet Choker

### Establishment `power` `rare` `cost 1` `tier A`

_tags_: innate

**Effect.** Whenever a card is Retained, reduce its cost by 1 this combat.

**Upgrade.** Innate. Whenever a card is Retained, reduce its cost by 1 this combat.

**Notes.** Since there are so many top tier cards with the retain keyword, establishment can be an exceptionally strong power. It is cheap to play and pays for itself within one or two turns generally. It works brilliantly with battle hymn as a consistent source of free damage. Although this card is rated highly, do make sure that your deck has a decent number of retain cards, otherwise this power will be worthless.

## Tier A-

### Protect `skill` `common` `cost 2` `tier A-`

_tags_: retain, dblock

**Effect.** Retain. Gain 12 Block.

**Upgrade.** Retain. Gain 16 Block.

**Notes.** Protect is a very solid source of block. It is not uncommon to draw more block cards than you need on a turn, and in these cases being able to retain a block card it's very useful for preventing chip damage. The block amount is decent for the energy cost. This card is particularly important versus encounters with hard hitting attacks, such as the large slime and the Guardian bosses. I would always be happy to have a couple of copies of this in my deck.

**Synergies.** Madness, Normality, Establishment, Spirit Shield, Wave of the Hand, Bronze Scales, Mercury Hourglass, Runic Dodecahedron, Toxic Egg, Calipers, Thread and Needle, Torii, Snecko Eye, Velvet Choker, Turnip, Pocketwatch, Cloak Clasp

### Ragnarok `attack` `rare` `cost 3` `tier A-`

_tags_: multihit

**Effect.** Deal 5 damage to a random enemy 5 times.

**Upgrade.** Deal 6 damage to a random enemy 6 times.

**Notes.** Ragnarok can be a powerful card in the right circumstances, but more often than not it is difficult to play. Against single target encounters, it can be extremely powerful since it has focused fire. On the other hand, against a boss like DECA and donate, the split damage can be the difference between winning and losing. Ragnarok has some very specific synergies: it benefits greatly from strength, wreath of flame effectively doubles the damage, and omniscience allows it to be played multiple times. 

**★ Super-synergies.** Omniscience, Swivel, Talk to the Hand, Wreath of Flame

### Talk to the Hand `attack` `uncommon` `cost 1` `tier A-`

_tags_: block

**Effect.** Deal 5 damage. Whenever you attack this enemy, gain 2 Block. Exhaust.

**Upgrade.** Deal 7 damage. Whenever you attack this enemy, gain 3 Block. Exhaust.

**Notes.** Talk to the hand is one of my favourite watcher cards. It allows you to play very offensively while still maintaining a defence. It synergises extremely well with itself, allowing you to generate upwards of 20 block just by using attacks every single turn. The effect is triggered multiple times by multi hit attacks, making cards like ragnarok very synergistic. Although in shorter combats with multiple low health enemies this card is less effective, it still acts as a source of damage and can be extremely effective versus elites and bosses. Note that since it is a debuff, bosses with transformations will be able to cleanse this effect. If I see talk to the hand, I almost always take it. 

**★ Super-synergies.** Ragnarok, Talk to the Hand

**Synergies.** Panic Button, The Bomb, Bowling Bash, Flying Sleeves, Tantrum, Wave of the Hand, Bronze Scales, Mercury Hourglass, Molten Egg, Runic Dodecahedron, Calipers, Thread and Needle, Torii, Stone Calendar

### Tantrum `attack` `uncommon` `cost 1` `tier A-`

_tags_: wrath, stance, multihit

**Effect.** Deal 3 damage 3 times. Enter Wrath. Shuffle this card into your draw pile.

**Upgrade.** Deal 3 damage 4 times. Enter Wrath. Shuffle this card into your draw pile.

**Notes.** Tantrum is an extremely powerful and consistent card. It is quite possibly the best source of wrath in the game. Since it shuffles back into your deck you can always guarantee that you will draw another source of wrath soon. It is not uncommon to play tantrum and draw it again the next turn. The upside of this is you are already in wrath, so tantrum’s damage is doubled. The fact that it hits multiple times means that it benefits greatly from strength. Note that sometimes decks choose to completely avoid wrath, particularly divinity decks. However, 90% of the time it is correct to have one or two copies of tantrum in your deck. This card is better than eruption in every possible sense, and it is common to remove eruption after drafting tantrum. 

**★ Super-synergies.** Halt

**Synergies.** J.A.X., Empty Fist, Flurry Of Blows, Meditate, Mental Fortress, Rushdown, Talk to the Hand, Wish, Wreath of Flame, Vajra, Molten Egg, Shuriken, Sling of Courage, Violet Lotus, Akabeko

**Anti-synergies.** Pressure Points, Signature Move, Snecko Eye

## Tier B+

### Conclude `attack` `uncommon` `cost 1` `tier B+`

_tags_: aoe, endturn

**Effect.** Deal 12 damage to ALL enemies. End your turn.

**Upgrade.** Deal 16 damage to ALL enemies. End your turn.

**Notes.** Because conclude is so energy efficient, I often find myself picking this. It is fast damage that makes light work of hallway fights. Usually, the end turn effect isn't relevant as you can simply play it as your last card of the turn. On the other hand, This makes it difficult and risky to use in conjunction with wrath , as you must end the turn in wrath as well. Note that it also has terrible synergy with other end turn cards, so beware when taking multiple copies of conclude and other cards such as vault.

### Fear No Evil `attack` `uncommon` `cost 1` `tier B+`

_tags_: stance, calm

**Effect.** Deal 8 damage. If the enemy intends to Attack, enter Calm.

**Upgrade.** Deal 11 damage. If the enemy intends to Attack, enter Calm.

**Notes.** Fear no evil is a strong and consistent attack that allows you to cheaply enter calm. Its strength lies in its flexibility. Typically, you only want to enter calm and leave wrath when you are being attacked. The biggest downside of taking this card is sometimes forgetting that your target must be attacking in order to put you into calm. On more than one occasion, I have killed a small minion that is not attacking and left myself in wrath when I didn't mean to. 

**Synergies.** Flurry Of Blows, Mental Fortress, Molten Egg, Violet Lotus

**Anti-synergies.** Signature Move, Snecko Eye

### Flurry Of Blows `attack` `common` `cost 0` `tier B+`

**Effect.** Deal 4 damage. Whenever you change stances, return this from the discard pile to your hand.

**Upgrade.** Deal 6 damage. Whenever you change stances, return this from the discard pile to your hand.

**Notes.** Synergises with your starter cards. Note that the card has to be in your discard pile in order for it to return to your hand. If on the same turn you can draw a card which will re-shuffle your deck, make sure you play this card AFTER the reshuffle so that it goes straight into the new discard pile. This card works with Calm, Wrath, Divinity, and Exit. Strategies relying on this card can be difficult against the Time Lord and the final boss.

### Sands of Time `attack` `uncommon` `cost 4` `tier B+`

_tags_: retain

**Effect.** Retain. Deal 20 damage. When Retained, lower its cost by 1 this combat.

**Upgrade.** Retain. Deal 26 damage. When Retained, lower its cost by 1 this combat.

**Notes.** Sands of time is a slow card but one with a lot of damage. ## Tier B

### Deceive Reality `skill` `uncommon` `cost 1` `tier B`

_tags_: dblock, create, exhaust

**Effect.** Gain 4 Block. Add a Safety to your hand.

**Upgrade.** Gain 7 Block. Add a Safety to your hand.

**Notes.** The power of Deceive reality lies in the retain keyword on the block that it generates. Being able to store up a number of blocks with retain is sometimes exceptionally important, particularly against bosses such as the Guardian and the champ who have turns with scary amounts of burst damage. It is also still fine in hallway fights, as it means you have block when you need it and can otherwise draft more offensively. Note that this card is affected by dexterity twice.

### Devotion `power` `rare` `cost 1` `tier B`

_tags_: mantra, stance

**Effect.** At the start of your turn, gain 2 Mantra.

**Upgrade.** At the start of your turn, gain 3 Mantra.

**Notes.** Devotion fits very well into mantra heavy decks. It allows you to predict when you will proc divinity, as it does not rely on you drawing a mantra card. This can be devastating if you do not draw any attacks on the turn that this happens. Generally speaking, it is very energy efficient and easy to play without being punished and several of this power stacked can ensure that you enter divinity every single turn which is not to be underestimated.

_tags_: artifact

**Effect.** Gain 3 Strength. Gain 3 Dexterity. Gain 1 less [energy] at the start of each turn.

**Upgrade.** Gain 4 Strength. Gain 4 Dexterity. Gain 1 less [energy] at the start of each turn.

**Notes.** Wreath of flame is quite a difficult card to analyse but it definitely has more uses than it may first seem.
