## 5. Method lessons

- In-sample "oracle headroom" is selection noise; "the F26–F32 'oracle headroom' never existed". Only split-half honest estimates count.
- Do not compare V across post-action states that differ in act, HP or room type.
- Offline per-decision gain understates play gain when decisions compound, but this "does not license moving the bar after the fact".
- Report pooled gains after a fresh confirmation slice; the slice that triggers a keep overstates (A10 shrank by more than half at confirmation).
- A model fails off its training range or objective (ascension 0; keys).
- Pre-register before code and games, with predictions; the F39 predictions were all too low.
- Gate rules in use: arm at > +2 SE (and ≥ +0.01 for headroom); keep at dscore > 0 and P ≥ 0.9; near miss gets one same-size extension; F25 fresh 2,000-seed confirmation.
- Equal compute: sims per decision unchanged; when sims change, mean search actions per game must match (ratio ≤ 1.01, "the F19/F22 compute rule"). Longer runs from reaching more floors are allowed.
- Operations: 2-hour background limit (resumable generators), container restarts, ten-worker owner limit, time estimates taken from A20 are too short for A10/Heart, build in a separate directory while jobs run, gates bit-identical after every rebuild, leak test for every new combination, C++-vs-Python agreement before play, timestamps from `date`, never re-draw a fresh split.

## 6. Hints of a big untested lever

1. **Simulations per second is a first-order lever [inferred].** Evidence: A10 +0.076 then +0.063 per doubling with wall time proportional to sims; tree reuse off and a fresh searcher per decision. A several-fold cheaper or more sample-efficient search would be worth more than the whole path line.
2. **Heart conversion is flat near 50% across 1×/2×/4× search [inferred]** (0.48, 0.50, 0.50). The Heart fight looks limited by the deck brought to it, and no Heart-labelled deck, relic or rest decision study exists in range.
3. **"No headroom" is local, not global [inferred].** F33–F34 change one decision with the same downstream policy and 8 noisy playouts; the one decision type that got a learned correction gained more per run in play (+0.030, +0.050) than its per-decision offline estimate (+0.0133, +0.0113) suggested, which the source attributes to compounding. A coordinated change of the whole out-of-combat policy was never tested.
4. **A cheap playout policy would unlock run-level search [inferred].** Playout-based path choice was dropped only on cost (≈ 100×), and learned distillation saturated at M=8 labels and failed on the Heart for lack of data.
5. **Losses are concentrated in a few known bosses [inferred]** (Automaton, Collector, Awakened One, Champ, Time Eater, Donu and Deca, the Heart). Boss-specific preparation or search settings are not discussed in range; whether the upcoming boss is a model input is not stated in this material.
