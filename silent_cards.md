## Tier S

### Wraith Form `power` `rare` `cost 3` `tier S`

_tags_: intangible

**Effect.** Gain 2 Intangible. At the end of your turn, lose 1 Dexterity.

**Upgrade.** Gain 3 Intangible. At the end of your turn, lose 1 Dexterity.

**Notes.** Wraith Form is an extremely powerful card which has a very real downside. It is extremely effective in hallway fights where you do not expect to be fighting for a long time. When playing out boss fights, you typically don't want to play this card until you know that there are only three terms of combat left. Obviously it is impossible to know exactly when there are three turns of combat left but if you know the boss phases well enough you should be able to guess how long each flight will take you. Playing this card too rarely makes your defends do absolutely nothing and also really reduces the effectiveness of the rest of your blocks, and the silent will not last very long in these situations. The more wraith forms you have the better. If you are constantly intangible, whether or not you can block is completely irrelevant. A well timed nightmare can allow you to have 4 Wraith Forms in your deck with just one card. The other ways of negating the dexterity down effect are by having artifact ignore it or by using orange pellets, which cleanses the dexterity down over time and also cleanses any current negative dexterity stacks. Note that even when intangible you will still be taking chip damage which adds up overtime. For this reason some kind of plated armor effect such as thread and needle can really help you stay topped up.  

**★ Super-synergies.** After Image, Bullet Time, Caltrops, Nightmare, Well-Laid Plans, Wraith Form, Panacea, Apparition, The Bomb, Orichalcum, Incense Burner, Orange Pellets, Clockwork Souvenir, Dolly's Mirror

**Synergies.** Masterful Stab, Forethought, Bottled Tornado, Frozen Egg, Mummified Hand, Bird-Faced Urn, Gambling Chip, Thread and Needle, Snecko Eye, Velvet Choker, Stone Calendar, Pocketwatch, Tungsten Rod

## Tier S-

### Well-Laid Plans `power` `uncommon` `cost 1` `tier S-`

_tags_: prepare

**Effect.** At the end of your turn, Retain up to 1 card.

**Upgrade.** At the end of your turn, Retain up to 2 cards.

**Notes.** Well laid plans is an exceptionally powerful card for the silent. When it used to cost 0 energy it was an instapick, exceptionally easy to play in fights for a considerable upside. Now that it costs 1 energy it is actually worth considering in a fight whether or not the one energy could be spent more effectively. in a longer boss fight or elite fight it is usually worth playing other power, but in a small hallway fight for example against the bandits, that one energy is typically better spent on an attack or a block. I would always be happy to pick up one or perhaps 2 copies of well laid plans in my deck, since sometimes one copy can find itself on the bottom of your deck and you need to make sure that you draw one early to be able to set up devastating turns with Catalyst, Grand Finale, Phantasmal Killer, Wraith Form, and Nightmare, just to name a few. In the worst case scenario, you are still able to hold an important block card for a dangerous turn, such as a Panic Button.

**★ Super-synergies.** Nightmare, Outmaneuver, Phantasmal Killer, Sneaky Strike, Tactician, Wraith Form

**Synergies.** Bullet Time, Grand Finale, Piercing Wail, Ritual Dagger, Hand of Greed, Frozen Egg, Mummified Hand, Bird-Faced Urn, Gambling Chip

**Anti-synergies.** Unload, Snecko Eye

## Tier A+

### After Image `power` `rare` `cost 1` `tier A+`

_tags_: block, innate

**Effect.** Whenever you play a card, gain 1 Block.

**Upgrade.** Innate. Whenever you play a card, gain 1 Block.

**Notes.** After Image is one of the Silent’s many extremely powerful block options. The first copy is great, and every copy of After Image that you add beyond that just makes it better and better. This deck has obvious synergies with Shiv cards, with Blade Dance being the best example, but does not need to run shivs to be powerful. Strangely enough, this works quite well with Wraith form since it prevents the tiny amount of incoming damage without forcing you to commit to block cards. Note that X cost cards can be played with 0 energy just to trigger this effect. Be aware that dexterity does not increase the block amount, nor is it reduced by frail. This is a brilliant power for the Heart fight - Beat of Death deals damage every time you play a card, and After Image completely offsets that. Higher ascension has more Beat of Death damage, and the Heart can buff this damage too. On the other hand, 3+ after images make the passive virtually non-existent. 

**★ Super-synergies.** Blade Dance, Wraith Form

**Synergies.** Accuracy, Blur, Caltrops, Cloak And Dagger, Infinite Blades, Masterful Stab, Nightmare, Storm of Steel, Shiv, Panic Button, The Bomb, Bronze Scales, Frozen Egg, Mercury Hourglass, Mummified Hand, Runic Dodecahedron, Bird-Faced Urn, Calipers, Gambling Chip, Thread and Needle, Torii, Gremlin Visage, Stone Calendar, Ninja Scroll

### Noxious Fumes `power` `uncommon` `cost 1` `tier A+`

_tags_: poison, aoe

**Effect.** At the start of your turn, apply 2 Poison to ALL enemies.

**Upgrade.** At the start of your turn, apply 3 Poison to ALL enemies.

**Notes.** Noxious fumes is always a great pick in any silent deck. Even when you are not running a poison death, it is a slow but reliable way to whittle down high health enemies over multiple turns for just one energy. It is also exceptionally good at burning away stacks of artifact very cheaply which can be relevant versus Deca and Donu and also the Heart. Of all of the poison cards in the game, this is the one I would most want to pick if I was not fully investing in a poison deck. Obviously this also works very well in poison decks too. Having one or two noxious fumes in your deck is never a bad idea.

**★ Super-synergies.** Bane

_tags_: poison

**Effect.** Whenever an attack deals unblocked damage, apply 1 Poison.

**Notes.** The main problem with envenom is its high energy cost. It is a very slow acting source of damage and thus sometimes useless in hallway fights. ### Piercing Wail `skill` `common` `cost 1` `tier A`

_tags_: strengthdown, exhaust

**Effect.** ALL enemies lose 6 Strength for 1 turn. Exhaust.

**Upgrade.** ALL enemies lose 8 Strength for 1 turn. Exhaust.

**Notes.** Piercing Wail is one of the best common cards that the silent has. Although it might not look better than a simple defend, it affects multiple enemies and is even more effective versus multihit attacks (such as the Dagger Elite from Act 2 or the Heart’s 15-hit attack). Its only downside is that it exhausts, but to counteract this I would be happy to pick up two 3 copies of this card in my deck. Since the card is common, this is fairly easy to do. It is negated by artifact, but the simple fact that this card burns one artifact off each enemy for one energy means it is usually still worth it. Note that strength reduction is also more effective than block when you are vulnerable or frail.

## Tier A-

### Alchemize `skill` `rare` `cost 1→0` `tier A-`

_tags_: exhaust, potion

**Effect.** Obtain a random potion. Exhaust.

**Notes.** Alchemize is a unique and powerful skill that I would rarely skip but often loses out to better alternatives, particularly rare cards. This card has considerable synergy with Burst. At higher ascensions, you're only offered two potion slots unless you take Potion Belt which means you don't get to store up a great amount of potions for important elite or boss battles. Obviously, never take this if you have Sozu. On the other hand, Toy Ornithopter means consistent healing which is important to the Silent.  If I find I have a lot of energy, I take this card as I know I can afford to play it without taking too many risks, since the potion that you get is always random.

### Catalyst `skill` `uncommon` `cost 1` `tier A-`

_tags_: poison, exhaust

**Effect.** Double an enemy's Poison. Exhaust.

**Upgrade.** Triple an enemy's Poison. Exhaust.

**Notes.** Catalyst is perhaps the single highest damage card the silent has access to. Although it requires some setup, it can turn boss encounters into an absolute joke. Be wary of bosses who can remove their debuffs, such as the champ and the time eater, because otherwise you will have to begin stacking poison all over again. Having 2 copies of catalyst makes it much more consistent, as sometimes you draw the first copy too early before there is significant poison on the target, and sometimes when you are cycling through the deck for the second time you might find that you are waiting on it for too long. Also, the difference between three times as much poison and nine times as much poison is extremely significant. Catalyst is a brilliant target for nightmare for this reason. Make sure not to pull the trigger too early on this card, as tripling five damage is considerably worse than tripling 20 damage. 

**★ Super-synergies.** Bouncing Flask, Burst, Nightmare

**Synergies.** Bane, Catalyst, Corpse Explosion, Crippling Cloud, Deadly Poison, Envenom, Grand Finale, Noxious Fumes, Poisoned Stab, Sadistic Nature, Toxic Egg, Dead Branch, Strange Spoon, Snecko Skull, The Specimen, Twisted Funnel

## Tier B+

### Bouncing Flask `skill` `uncommon` `cost 2` `tier B+`

_tags_: poison

**Effect.** Apply 3 Poison to a random enemy 3 times.

**Upgrade.** Apply 3 Poison to a random enemy 4 times.

**Notes.** Bouncing flask is an important card in poison decks. Against single target enemies, the randomness of this card is fully negated. Since it is poison, it works well against targets with high health totals such as elites or bosses. The fact that it includes three or four applications of poison means that it is very useful for burning through multiple stacks of Artifact, for example against the Bronze Automaton. The fact that the targets are random makes this slightly more awkward in hallway fights, where it is difficult to guarantee where the poison will go. Sometimes, if this is in your opening hand, you should play it out first and then decide which targets to kill first based on where it landed.
